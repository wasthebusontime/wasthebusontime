What do these mean? {% if scope %}

Timepoints are the stops with times printed in the schedule. Drivers are expected not to leave them early. This matches how {{ agency.NAME }} measures on-time performance.

All stops: at stops between timepoints, the schedule's times are estimates, and buses aren't expected to keep to them exactly. A bus can show as early there without having left a timepoint early, so on-time numbers are lower for all stops.

Every number counts departures: one bus leaving one stop on one trip. A trip that passes five timepoints counts as four departures here, because the last stop of a trip has no departure.

{% endif %} {% if filter %}

Day type and Time of day show only departures on those days and at those times, each counted at the time it was scheduled to leave. Two charts keep the rest for comparison: By hour of the day follows only the day type, with the chosen time of day shaded, and By day type follows only the time of day, with the chosen day type highlighted. Choices with no scheduled service are grayed out. The trips line, how complete the data is, and the notes always cover every day and time.

Patterns

Besides measuring how late buses were, the site shows patterns: conditions that many late departures had in common, found in past trips by fixed rules. A pattern is an interpretation of the numbers, not a measurement, and is always shown in a panel labeled “Pattern”. The rules, their thresholds and their limits are under How we find patterns.

A carry-over pattern uses the previous trip on the same bus’s block. After a layover of less than {{ co.long_layover_s // 60 }} minutes, the bus’s arrival at the first stop is the time it was recorded arriving there, as for end-of-line arrivals. After a longer layover, it comes from the bus’s GPS positions at the end of the previous trip.

{% endmacro %} {# The data page's description of patterns.csv, when the stats have patterns. #} {% macro patterns_files(doc) %}

Patterns

patterns.csv has one row for each pattern on the Patterns page, with the counts behind it. It is rewritten every night, so a pattern can appear or go away as data comes in.

  • id stays the same from night to night. kind is carry_over. stops is the trip’s first stop. daytype and band are all for a general pattern, otherwise the day type and time of day it is for. rank is its place in the route’s list.
  • late counts the late departures behind it, matched those on trips that left their first stop late right after a carry-over, and share is matched divided by late. trips counts the trips with a late departure, and days the days with a matched trip.
  • previous is where the bus came from on the matched trips: the route and direction of the previous trip on the block, with the matched late departures behind each, as route:direction_id:matched, largest first and separated by spaces.

The rules are under How we find patterns.
